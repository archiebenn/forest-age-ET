\section{Aims and Objectives}
The overarching aim of this research project is to investigate how the age of a forest may impact predictions of \ac{ET} using \ac{ML} in forest ecosystems. The stand age of a forest has been shown to impact \ac{ET} dynamics \parencite{oishi2020}, and therefore could alter how ML models form predictions of \ac{ET} if age is included as a feature during training and inference. 
\par Given the importance of predicting \ac{ET} \parencite{amani2023review, liyew2025review} and the lack of infrastructure to measure \ac{ET} directly with eddy covariance (EC) methods in many ecoregions \parencite{hill2017}, the first part of this project will focus on spatial generalisation, asking: \textit{``If a model is built using readily available data from one set of sites, how well can it form predictions of \ac{ET} rates at a geographically unseen site, and how does utilising forest age affect predictions?"}. To address this question, FLUXNET sites with forests aged by \textcite{besnard2018} are used alongside a framework developed to quantify site similarities based on characteristics. \ac{RF} models were trained with and without forest age as a feature, and predictions of \ac{ET} rates from both directly compared at unseen sites with ground-truth validation provided by EC measurements.
%\par Estimating future \ac{ET} rates could also be useful in shaping policies and water use management over time at a single location. The second part of this project aims to address the temporal generalisation of \ac{ET} models with and without age as a feature, asking \textit{``If a model is built using historic data, does knowledge of any existing age-\ac{ET} relationship aid in the accuracy of future \ac{ET} predictions?"}. To address this question, site-specific models are trained with and without age as a feature on the earliest 80\% of site data, and tested against the most recent 20\% using ground-truth validation provided by EC measurements. Time series decomposition can then be used to identify any underlying shapes in the form of these \ac{ET} predictions over time, with both models compared in section \ref{sec:temporal_res}.
\par The objectives of this project are twofold: (1) To assess the use of forest age in models for spatial predictions of \ac{ET}, and (2) to outline future directions for research in utilising forest age in \ac{ET} predictions.
